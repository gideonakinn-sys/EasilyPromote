# Content & UX Writing Guidelines (dashboard)

> **Living document.** Scope: **dashboard content only** (labels, headers, helper text, tooltips, empty states, errors, buttons, wizard steps) — not the marketing site. Update this file as new patterns are established, and consult it whenever writing or reviewing user-facing dashboard copy.

## 1. Core principle

- Content is part of the product experience, not decoration on top of it.
- Every piece of copy should answer a real question the user has at that moment — not describe the mechanism behind a feature.
- Write for the person's goal, not the system's logic.

## 2. Compliance-critical rules (non-negotiable)

- **Never use "escrow"** or any language implying business payment is contingent on verified results — prohibited by Paystack and compliance requirements.
- Business payment must always be framed as an **upfront fee to EasilyPromote**.
- Creator payment is **separate and performance-based**.
- These two payment flows must **never** be conflated or blurred in the same sentence — or in nearby copy.

## 3. Voice and tone

- Emotionally direct, not corporate. Prefer plain, warm, human phrasing over formal or legalistic language.
- Write like you're explaining something to a smart friend, not documenting a spec.
- Avoid internal/system jargon leaking into user-facing text (e.g. "price table," "referral tracking webhook") — translate mechanism into outcome language.

## 4. Framing rules

- Lead with the user's intent, not the field's mechanics. e.g. ask "Where are your customers?" — not "Audience Locations".
- When a field's purpose isn't obvious from its label, add a one-line helper that states what it does or why it matters — not a restatement of the label.
- When copy would raise a question in the reader's mind ("why does this box say ₦0?", "what happens if I don't fill this in?"), answer that question in the copy itself rather than leaving it implicit.
- Prefer showing real, calculated numbers over vague reassurances — except where a number cannot be shown for legitimate reasons (e.g. a rate that isn't fixed). In that case, be honest that it varies rather than implying a false precision.

## 5. Structural rules

- Simplicity wins: fewer steps, fewer fields, fewer words — favor consolidation over exhaustive coverage. If two pieces of copy say the same thing from different angles, cut one.
- Don't explain a mechanism twice (once in an intro paragraph, once again in a field-level helper). Choose the one place it's actually needed.
- Every screen should differentiate real product advantages where relevant — e.g. pre-publish approval is a trust/safety differentiator and should be framed as such wherever it appears, not buried as a neutral process step.

## 6. Consistency rules

- The same concept must use the same name everywhere. If a budget is called "Referral Budget" in one field, it cannot be called "Performance Budget" in the summary below it.
- Currency values should never show sub-unit decimals (e.g. no "₦186,666.2").
- Pill/label sets covering a shared concept (e.g. campaign type names, rank tiers) must follow one grammatical pattern — all verb-led, or all noun-led — not mixed.

## 7. When in doubt

- Ask: *"If a business owner with no context read only this line, would they know what to do and why?"* If not, rewrite it.
- Prefer cutting a sentence over adding a caveat.
